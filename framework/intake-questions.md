# Intake questions

What Claude asks at the start of a mandate, before any analysis. Asked as tappable multiple choice cards, up to four at a time, business questions first. Sid can type or dictate an answer instead of picking one.

Five rounds. Rounds 1 and 2 are always asked. Rounds 3 to 5 are asked only where the answer is not already clear.

If an answer is not known yet, "not decided" is a valid answer and the analysis proceeds on stated assumptions.

---

## Round 1. The business

1. What is being made or built, and what is the output measured in? Tonnes a year, megawatts, megawatts of IT load, rooms, units.
2. Where does this sit in the value chain? Raw material, intermediate, finished product, assembly, services.
3. Where will it sell? Mostly inside the state, mostly elsewhere in India, mostly export, or a mix.
4. What dominates the cost base? Power, labour, raw material, logistics, capital.

Round 1 decides which instruments can possibly matter. A power-heavy project and a labour-heavy project want completely different things from a state.

---

## Round 2. The project

1. What is the total project cost, and roughly how does it split across land, building, plant and machinery?
2. How many people will it employ once it is running?
3. When does construction start and when is commercial production expected?
4. Is this a new unit, an expansion of an existing unit, a diversification, a modernisation, or a revival?

The last one matters more than it looks. Expansion is tested differently from a new unit in every state policy, and the difference is often the whole benefit.

---

## Round 3. Location

1. Is the state already decided, shortlisted to a few, or genuinely open?
2. Is a specific site locked, or is the district still open within the state?
3. What constrains the location? Port access, raw material source, grid connectivity, water, skilled labour, proximity to customers.
4. Does the group already have units in any of the states in question?

An existing unit in a state changes the question from new unit to expansion, and sometimes removes eligibility altogether.

---

## Round 4. Where things stand

1. Has any land been bought, allotted or an agreement signed?
2. Has any application, registration or memorandum of understanding been filed with any state?
3. Has anything been announced publicly, at an investor summit or otherwise?
4. Has any spend already been incurred on this project, and from what date?

This round exists because of the clocks. Spend incurred before the right date, or an announcement made before registration, can quietly cost the benefit.

---

## Round 5. The ask

1. What is the actual question? Which state to choose, what the package is worth, how to structure the investment, how to negotiate, or how to claim and protect what is already sanctioned.
2. Who is this for? Promoter, chief financial officer, board, or an internal view.
3. By when is it needed?
4. Is there an existing offer or draft package on the table that should be tested?

Round 5 decides the output format and the depth. Everything before it decides the content.

---

## What happens after intake

1. Claude checks the library for every state and every layer in scope, and says plainly what is already decoded and what is missing.
2. Missing policies are fetched from government sources and decoded into the library in the standard format.
3. The value bridge is built on the project's own numbers.
4. The idea levers are run.
5. The output is delivered page by page, with sign-off after page one.

Where a policy cannot be verified from a government source, that is stated rather than filled in.
