# Output templates

The outward formats. What Sid actually receives, and in what order. The decode template is the inward format, how a policy is written into the library. This file is the outward one.

All outputs are downloadable HTML files, opened in Safari. Never published artifacts.

**Common skeleton for every output.** Key takeaway box at the top. Visuals before text. Certainty tags of fact, interpretation, market view or evolving. A source link on every verified point. Abbreviations decoded inline at first use. Date of preparation on the page.

Multi-page outputs are delivered page by page. Page one goes out, sign-off is taken, then the rest follows.

---

## 1. Policy one-pager

Sent after every decode. One screen of real content, not a summary of the whole policy.

| Block | What is on it |
|---|---|
| Takeaway box | What this policy is worth, to whom, and the one thing that decides it |
| The three clocks | Policy validity, application deadline, benefit tenure, as a timeline bar |
| What it gives | The instrument table: instrument, base, rate, cap, tenure |
| Who qualifies | A short decision tree, not a paragraph |
| What it does not say | The grey areas, stated as questions |
| Risks | Clawback triggers and payment record |
| Sources | Government links with date accessed |

---

## 2. State comparison matrix

Used whenever more than two states are in play.

States as rows. Criteria as columns, drawn from the state selection criteria file. Every cell marked verified or inferred. Wide format, so it scrolls sideways rather than stacking.

Two columns always appear and always last: effective yield, and certainty of payment as high, medium or low. Those two are the decision, and everything to their left is the working.

---

## 3. Value bridge sheet

One page. The bridge table from headline entitlement down to effective yield as a percentage of total project cost, with the assumption behind each haircut stated on the same line. A cash arrival profile by year underneath it.

Written so that a chief financial officer can see in one minute where the headline number went.

---

## 4. Mandate report

Page by page, in this order, with sign-off after page one.

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Summary and recommendation |
| 2 | The project as we understood it, so any wrong assumption surfaces early |
| 3 | The policy universe across all five layers, as a map |
| 4 | Eligibility, as a decision tree |
| 5 | The value bridge |
| 6 | State comparison, where location is open |
| 7 | The top five ideas, each with the four-chair test |
| 8 | Risks, clawback and what has to be watched |
| 9 | What to do next, and in what order |

Mandate reports are never saved in the repository.

---

## 5. Brief

The monthly and fortnightly routine outputs. What changed, and what it means for Sid's sectors. Short. Every item carries a date and a source, and says plainly whether it is notified, approved or only announced.

---

## 6. Explainer

Learning pieces. Diagram heavy, built for someone coming to the subject cold. Flow diagrams, decision logic and matrices carry the explanation, with prose only where a diagram cannot do the work.

---

## Where outputs live

Library outputs, meaning one-pagers, comparisons, briefs and explainers, are saved in `outputs/`. Mandate reports are not saved anywhere in the repository.
