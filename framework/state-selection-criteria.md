# State selection criteria

How states are compared when a project is open on location. The comparison is on **effective yield multiplied by certainty of payment**, never on headline percentage.

Delivered as a wide matrix with states as rows and the criteria below as columns. Every cell marked verified or inferred, with a source link on anything verified.

---

## A. What the money is worth

| Criterion | What is recorded |
|---|---|
| Effective yield | Output of the value bridge, as a percentage of total project cost |
| Instrument fit | Whether the instruments on offer match what this project actually generates |
| Absorption headroom | Whether the project can generate enough base to absorb a tax-linked entitlement |
| Binding cap | Which cap governs, and how long the entitlement takes to pay out |
| Customised package route | Whether a bespoke package is available, which committee approves it, and what is known of past packages |

## B. Whether the money arrives

| Criterion | What is recorded |
|---|---|
| Disbursement record | Observed lag between claim and payment, with evidence and date |
| Pending arrears | Known backlog, from budget documents, audit reports or assembly answers |
| Budget provision | Whether the scheme has a dedicated line and how it has moved |
| Self-funding or budget cash | Tax-linked support returns part of what the state collected. Capital subsidy is cash out. The first is usually safer |
| Agreement quality | Whether the state signs a written agreement, and whether it carries a stability clause |
| Amendment history | Whether the state has cut benefits mid-term before |

## C. Whether the project can actually be built

| Criterion | What is recorded |
|---|---|
| Land | Availability, readiness, title, whether in a government estate, time to possession |
| Power | Cost per unit for industrial consumers, reliability, open access rules, captive and renewable banking terms |
| Water and effluent | Availability and consent position |
| Logistics | Distance to port, rail siding, highway, and what that costs per tonne |
| Labour | Availability of the skills this project needs, and the cost |
| Ecosystem | Existing suppliers, anchor units, testing and support infrastructure |

## D. How hard it is to deal with the state

| Criterion | What is recorded |
|---|---|
| Nodal agency | Which agency, and its observed performance |
| Approval speed | Single window performance, time to consents |
| Institutional route | District, state committee or Cabinet, and what that means for timelines |
| Officials | Relevant office holders, always date-stamped |
| Policy stability | Whether commitments have carried across changes of government. The test is continuity of commitments, never party preference |

---

## How to score

Do not produce a single composite score. It hides the trade-off that matters.

Produce two numbers for each state. **Effective yield**, from the value bridge. **Certainty**, as a band of high, medium or low, with the reasoning stated. Then plot or tabulate the two together.

A state offering a high headline with a weak payment record, and a state offering a lower headline that pays on time, are a genuine choice and the promoter should make it with both numbers in front of them.

---

## What is usually decisive

In practice the decision turns on three things more often than on the headline rate.

Whether the project can absorb a tax-linked entitlement given its sales mix. Whether land is actually ready, because delay costs more than most subsidies are worth. And whether the state has paid people on time recently.

Record those three first in every comparison.
