Viksit Gujarat Industrial Policy 2026

State generic layer · Gujarat · Policy one-pager · Prepared 13 November 2026

Read this first — sourcing

The Industries Commissionerate website blocks automated access and its policy page still lists the 2020 policy. The Investor Facilitation Portal lists only the 2020 and 2015 policies.

Two government sources have been reached. A Press Information Bureau release confirms the announcement date of 15 June 2026, the Industries Commissioner, and that 21 key sectors were identified. iNDEXTb — the Gujarat government's own Industrial Extension Bureau — hosts the Government Resolutions, and the indexed text of the taluka classification Resolution has confirmed the taluka split and its direction.

The incentive rate tables below still rest on professional-firm reproductions and are marked inferred cell by cell. Do not quote those rates to a client before the iNDEXTb copy is read.

The takeaway

What it is worth. A headline ceiling of 15% to 50% of eligible fixed capital investment, as a mix the unit chooses for itself between capital subsidy, 7% interest subsidy and power tariff support. On a thrust-sector Mega project the 35% headline works out at roughly 26% of project cost in nominal rupees and roughly 16% in present value, before any discount for payment risk. interpretation

To whom. Best fit is a power-heavy or export-heavy thrust-sector project, or anything in the five selected thrust sectors, which carry the top 50% ceiling at any size including MSME. Weakest fit is a land-heavy or general-sector project.

The one thing that decides it. Not the headline percentage — the annual ceiling. Each tier carries both a percentage cap per year and an absolute rupee cap, and unused annual entitlement cannot be carried forward. Above roughly ₹35,700 crore of eligible investment on an Ultra Mega project, the absolute cap alone turns a 40% headline into an effective 30%.

"Choose Your Incentive" — the policy's best feature. The unit selects its own mix of the three components rather than taking a fixed package. A power-heavy project weights toward power tariff; a leveraged project toward interest subsidy; an equity-funded project toward capital subsidy. interpretation

What is not here. There is no state GST reimbursement instrument. Gujarat has moved away from the net state GST refund model that several competing states still run. An export-heavy or inter-state-heavy project is better off for this; a large intra-state domestic seller loses the instrument that would pay it best elsewhere. verify — a significant absence

Who qualifies

1 · Will commercial production start between 1 June 2026 and 31 May 2031?
No, already producing before 1 June 2026 → governed by the earlier scheme, cannot opt in. · Not yet producing but under the old policy → election available, within six months of the Government Resolution, and the election is final.
2 · Which sector tier does the product sit in?
Sports goods, toys, footwear, robots or drones → selected thrust 50% / 45% at any size, including MSME. · One of the 16 thrust sectors → thrust. · Anything else → general 20% / 15%, and apparently capped at Large size.
3 · What is the gross fixed capital investment, and are the jobs there?
₹125 cr+ → Large 35% / 25% if thrust. · ₹1,000 cr+ and 250 jobs (+50 per further ₹200 cr), thrust only → Mega 35% / 30%. · ₹10,000 cr+ and 3,000 jobs (+500 per further ₹5,000 cr), thrust only → Ultra Mega 40% / 35%.
4 · Which taluka category is the site in?
Category A (130 talukas, the less developed set) carries the higher slab; Category B (138) the lower. Almost every flagship destination is Category B — Dahej (Vagra), Ankleshwar, Jhagadia, Sanand, Mundra, Anjar, Gandhidham, Morbi, and the four big cities. Dholera is the one major destination on the top band. Worth 5 to 10 percentage points of eligible investment with no change to the project.
5 · Above ₹1,000 cr in a thrust sector?
A customised package is available from a High Power Committee chaired by the Chief Minister, which can also decide matters the Government Resolution does not cover. For any project of this size the published policy is the floor, not the ceiling.

The annual cap trap

An Ultra Mega thrust project in Category A: ceiling 40%, annual cap 3.5%, absolute cap ₹1,250 crore a year, 12 years. On ₹50,000 crore of eligible investment, 3.5% is ₹1,750 crore — but the absolute cap holds the draw to ₹1,250 crore. The entitlement at 40% is ₹20,000 crore; what can actually be drawn is ₹15,000 crore. The 40% headline becomes an effective 30%, and the shortfall is lost rather than deferred because unused annual amounts cannot be carried forward. The crossover sits at roughly ₹35,700 crore of eligible investment. Above that, every further rupee earns a lower effective rate.

What it does not say

  1. Which taluka category do Halol, Hazira, Vapi, Savli and Padra sit in? The rest of the industrial map is now mapped — these are the remaining gaps, along with the specific GIDC estates on any live site list.
  2. What are the thrust sub-categories within the 16 sectors? The count is settled at 16 thrust plus 5 selected thrust. What is not settled is whether a specific product is found inside a sub-category — which is the difference between a 20% and a 35% ceiling.
  3. Is the thrust test applied at unit level or product level? A unit making both a thrust and a general product needs to know whether it classifies as a whole or apportions. This is where most incentive litigation starts.
  4. Can a general-sector project reach Mega status? As read, Mega and Ultra Mega appear open only to thrust sectors — which would cap a ₹15,000 crore general-sector project at 20%.
  5. What does "maximum two availments" mean? Two projects, or two claims? This governs whether phasing is available as a structuring move.
  6. Does the entitlement survive a merger, demerger or slump sale? Not identified. Decides whether the incentive is an asset in a transaction.
  7. When exactly does the six-month election clock expire? Six months from the Government Resolution of 8 September 2026 gives roughly 8 March 2027 — live, and irreversible.
  8. What is the SCALE Committee? Referred to in the policy, not explained.
  9. Is there really no state GST instrument? Absences are worth confirming rather than assuming.

Risks

RiskPosition
Clawback rate18% per annum on amounts already disbursed, plus forfeiture of undisbursed amounts. Punitive — makes continuing conditions worth monitoring actively, not annually.
Local employment conditionAt least 85% of the overall workforce and 60% of managerial and supervisory staff must be Gujarat-domiciled, continuously, for the whole incentive period — with 18% clawback behind it. For a project importing specialist or expatriate staff, the 85% overall test binds, not the 60% managerial one. Model it against the hiring plan before commissioning. interpretation
Continuous productionRequired through the incentive period, with relief only for events beyond management's control.
Instrument strengthA government resolution is an executive order the department can amend, not a gazetted notification. For a project relying on a 12-year entitlement, a stability commitment is something to seek in the package rather than assume. interpretation
Payment recordUnknown. The policy is too new for any claim cycle to have completed, and the record under the 2015 and 2020 policies has not yet been gathered. Gujarat runs a revenue-surplus budget and has honoured industrial commitments across changes of government market view — a reasonable prior, not evidence.
Short claim windowsThree months for the provisional certificate, three months for the stamp duty claim. The stamp duty window is easy to miss where land was bought years before production started.
Stacking capState and central incentives combined cannot exceed eligible fixed capital investment. Generous as stacking caps go, but it is there. interpretation
