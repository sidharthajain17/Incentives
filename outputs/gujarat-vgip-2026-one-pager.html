Viksit Gujarat Industrial Policy 2026

Gujarat's general state industrial policy · Policy one-pager · Prepared 13 November 2026

The sourcing position, stated at the outset

Gujarat's Industries Commissionerate website blocks automated access, and its published policy page still lists the superseded 2020 policy. The state's Investor Facilitation Portal does not carry the 2026 policy at all. Readers should therefore know exactly how much weight each figure below carries.

Two government sources have been reached and are relied upon. A Press Information Bureau release confirms the policy's announcement date, names the Industries Commissioner and states that 21 sectors were identified. More usefully, iNDEXTb — the Gujarat government's own Industrial Extension Bureau — hosts all three Government Resolutions, and the text of its taluka classification Resolution has confirmed both the classification and its direction.

The incentive rate tables, however, still rest on professional-firm reproductions of the Government Resolution, and are marked as unverified cell by cell. They should not be quoted to a client until read against the iNDEXTb copy. Everything else is tagged according to the strength of its source.

What a CFO needs to take from this policy

What it is worth. The policy offers a combined entitlement of between 15% and 50% of eligible fixed capital investment — the project cost that the state actually recognises for subsidy, which is materially smaller than the total project cost because land and several other items are excluded. Importantly, the unit chooses its own mix between capital subsidy, interest subsidy and power tariff support. On a typical thrust-sector project of ₹1,000 crore or more, a 35% headline entitlement is worth in the region of 26% of total project cost in nominal terms, and closer to 16% once the delay in receiving it is discounted. interpretation

Where it pays best. The policy suits a power-intensive or export-oriented project in one of the 16 thrust sectors, and it is exceptionally generous to the five sectors the state has singled out — sports goods, toys, footwear, robots and drones — which draw the top 50% entitlement at any size, including the smallest. It suits a land-heavy project, or one outside the thrust list, considerably less well.

The two things that decide the real number. First, where the project sits: the state has placed almost every established industrial address on the lower band, and Dholera is the one major destination on the higher one. Second, the annual ceiling: each tier caps what may be drawn in any single year, both as a percentage and in absolute rupees, and unused entitlement cannot be carried forward. Neither point appears in the headline rate, and both are discussed below.

The three clocks

Policies of this kind run on three separate deadlines, which are routinely confused with one another. A policy that remains valid until 2031 is of no use to a unit that has missed its registration deadline. All three are set out here, together with a fourth that governs how much capital expenditure actually counts.

What the policy gives

The policy's distinctive feature is flexibility. Rather than handing a unit a fixed package, it allows the unit to assemble its own combination of three instruments, subject to a sub-limit on each and an overall ceiling on the total. "Choose your incentive" — the policy's strongest feature

Allowing the unit to set its own mix is unusual among Indian state policies, and it is genuinely valuable. A power-intensive project may weight its entitlement toward power tariff support, which arrives month by month through the operating life. A highly leveraged project may weight it toward interest subsidy. A project funded largely from equity, having little interest cost to subsidise, may take the whole entitlement as capital subsidy. interpretation

The practical consequence is that the optimal mix differs from project to project, and should be modelled on a present-value basis rather than selected by headline percentage. That comparison is worth building once as a reusable tool.

What the policy does not contain, and why it matters

There is no state GST reimbursement instrument. Gujarat moved away from reimbursing state goods and services tax in its 2020 policy, replacing it with capital subsidy, and the 2026 policy continues that approach. Several competing states still run reimbursement models.

This cuts in both directions, and it is the sharpest point of comparison between Gujarat and its peers. An export-oriented or largely inter-state seller is better off here, because its state GST collections would have been modest and the Gujarat entitlement does not depend on them. A large seller into the Gujarat market is worse off, having lost the instrument that would have paid it best elsewhere, and must take its value through capital subsidy instead. A significant absence, and absences warrant confirmation rather than assumption

Who qualifies

Eligibility runs through five successive tests, and each one narrows the entitlement. The sector test and the taluka test together account for most of the variation in outcome.

1 · Will commercial production begin between 1 June 2026 and 31 May 2031?
A unit already in production before 1 June 2026 is governed by the earlier scheme and cannot opt into this one. A unit not yet in production but holding entitlements under the old policy may elect between the two, within six months of the Government Resolution — and that election is final.
2 · Which sector tier does the product fall into?
Sports goods, toys, footwear, robots or drones fall in the selected thrust tier 50% / 45%, available at any size including the smallest. One of the 16 thrust sectors attracts the middle band. Anything else is general 20% / 15% and, on the reading of the thresholds, cannot reach mega status however large the investment.
3 · What is the investment, and are the jobs there?
₹125 crore or more in plant and machinery qualifies as large. ₹1,000 crore with 250 direct jobs — rising by 50 jobs for every further ₹200 crore — qualifies as mega, in thrust sectors only. ₹10,000 crore with 3,000 direct jobs, rising by 500 for every further ₹5,000 crore, qualifies as ultra mega. The escalating job requirement is easily overlooked and compounds on very large projects: a ₹20,000 crore ultra mega project carries 4,000 jobs, not 3,000.
4 · Which taluka category is the site in?
Category A, comprising 130 talukas, carries the higher band; Category B, comprising 138, the lower. Almost every flagship industrial destination is Category B — Dahej, Ankleshwar, Jhagadia, Sanand, Mundra, Anjar, Gandhidham, Morbi and all four major cities. Dholera is the one major destination on the top band. The difference is worth 5 to 10 percentage points of eligible investment with no change whatever to the project.
5 · Is the project above ₹1,000 crore in a thrust sector?
If so, a customised package is available from a High Power Committee chaired by the Chief Minister, which is also empowered to decide matters the Government Resolution does not address. For any project of this scale, the published policy should be treated as the floor rather than the ceiling.

What counts as eligible investment

This definition determines the real value of the entitlement far more than the headline percentage does. Two exclusions do most of the damage: land is excluded altogether, and buildings are valued at the government's own rate card rather than at what they actually cost to construct.

The annual ceiling, which is where large projects lose most

Consider an ultra mega thrust-sector project in a Category A taluka. Its combined ceiling is 40% of eligible investment, drawn over twelve years, but no more than 3.5% of eligible investment and no more than ₹1,250 crore may be drawn in any single year.

On eligible investment of ₹50,000 crore, 3.5% would be ₹1,750 crore — so the absolute cap of ₹1,250 crore binds instead. The nominal entitlement at 40% is ₹20,000 crore; what can actually be drawn across twelve years is ₹15,000 crore. A 40% headline therefore becomes an effective 30%, and because unused annual entitlement cannot be carried forward, the shortfall is lost rather than merely deferred.

The crossover point is worth committing to memory: the absolute cap begins to bind on an ultra mega project once eligible investment passes roughly ₹35,700 crore. Below that figure the percentage cap governs; above it, every additional rupee invested earns a progressively lower effective rate. For projects of that scale the customised package route is not an optimisation — it is the answer.

What the policy does not say

These are set out as questions because that is how they should be put — either to the department, or into the negotiation of a customised package. Three questions from the first draft of this decode have since been resolved and have been removed.

  1. Is the thrust-sector test applied to the unit as a whole, or product by product? A unit manufacturing both a thrust product and a general one needs to know whether it is classified in its entirety or whether the entitlement is apportioned. The policy as read does not say. In our experience this is where most incentive disputes begin.
  2. Can a general-sector project reach mega status at all? On the reading of the thresholds, mega and ultra mega status appear confined to thrust sectors — which would hold a ₹15,000 crore general-sector project to the large general band of 20%. That is a substantial commercial consequence resting on an interpretation.
  3. What does the limit of "two availments" during the policy period actually mean? Two separate projects, or two claims? The answer governs whether phasing a large investment into distinct units is available as a structuring option at all.
  4. Does the entitlement survive a merger, demerger or slump sale? The policy as read does not address transferability. This determines whether an incentive entitlement is an asset capable of being valued in a transaction, and it is the most commercially significant gap remaining.
  5. What constitutes a qualifying expansion? No threshold test has been identified. The 2020 policy expressed this as a percentage increase in gross fixed capital investment, and a similar approach is likely.
  6. Which taluka category applies to Halol, Hazira, Vapi, Savli and Padra? The rest of the industrial map has been established; these remain open, along with the specific GIDC estates on any live site list.
  7. What are the sub-categories within each of the 16 thrust sectors? The count is now settled. What is not settled is whether a particular product is found within a sub-category — and that is the difference between a 20% and a 35% ceiling.
  8. When precisely does the six-month election period expire? Six months from the Government Resolution of 8 September 2026 points to approximately 8 March 2027. For any client commissioning a project around now, this is a live and irreversible deadline.
  9. What is the mandate of the SCALE Committee? It is constituted under the policy but not explained.

Risks and conditions

The conditions attaching to this entitlement are continuing rather than one-off, and the recovery provisions are severe. They warrant active monitoring rather than an annual review.

RiskPosition, and what follows from it
Recovery on breachAmounts already disbursed are recoverable with interest at 18% a year, and undisbursed amounts are forfeited. At that rate, a breach discovered late is materially more expensive than the entitlement was worth, which is the reason the conditions below deserve monitoring in real time.
Local employment85% of the overall workforce and 60% of managerial and supervisory staff must be domiciled in Gujarat, continuously, throughout the incentive period. For a project bringing in specialist or expatriate technical staff, it is the 85% overall test that binds rather than the 60% managerial one. We would recommend modelling this against the hiring plan before commissioning. interpretation
Continuous productionRequired throughout the incentive period, with relief available only for events genuinely beyond the control of management.
Strength of the instrumentThe entitlement rests on a Government Resolution — an executive order of the department — rather than on a gazetted notification, and the department may amend it. Where a project depends on a twelve-year entitlement, a stability commitment is something to be sought within a customised package rather than assumed from the published policy. interpretation
Payment recordNot yet quantified. The policy is too recent for any claim cycle to have completed, and the record under the 2015 and 2020 policies has still to be measured. Gujarat runs a revenue-surplus budget and has honoured industrial commitments across changes of government market view, which is a reasonable starting assumption but is not evidence. Certainty of payment is accordingly carried at medium rather than high.
Short claim windowsThree months for the provisional eligibility certificate, and three months for the stamp duty claim. The stamp duty window is easily missed on projects where land was acquired years before production began.
Limit on combined supportState and central incentives taken together cannot exceed eligible fixed capital investment. As caps on combining schemes go this is generous, and leaves real headroom for a central scheme above a 35% state entitlement — but it is there. interpretation
