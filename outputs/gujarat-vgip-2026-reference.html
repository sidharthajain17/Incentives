Viksit Gujarat Industrial Policy 2026

Gujarat's general industrial incentive framework, running from 1 June 2026 to 31 May 2031. It offers between 15% and 50% of a project's recognised capital cost, in a mix the investor chooses, and it decides most of that outcome on two things: what the unit makes, and which taluka it stands in.
Decoded from Government of Gujarat, Industries and Mines Department, Resolution No. IMD/WRT/e-file/9/2026/2320/I (Scheme for assistance to Large, Mega and Ultra Mega Industries) and Resolution No. IMD/HMR/e-file/9/2026/2211/I (Classification of talukas), both approved 8 September 2026.

01

The policy, and the clocks that govern it

Gujarat announced this policy on 15 June 2026 under the framing Viksit Gujarat se Viksit Bharat. It follows the Gujarat Industrial Policy 2020. For the purposes of this scheme the predecessor is narrower: the Resolution defines the previous scheme as the two Aatmanirbhar Gujarat schemes of 5 October 2022, one for Large Industries and Thrust Sector and one for Mega Industries. Four separate time limits govern an entitlement under it, and they are routinely confused with one another. A policy that remains valid until 2031 is of no use to a unit that has missed its registration deadline, and three deadlines are running now.

How the policy is built

The policy document sets direction. The money rules sit in Government Resolutions issued by the Industries and Mines Department. A Government Resolution is an executive order of the department. It is easier for the state to amend than a gazetted notification. That matters when a project is relying on a twelve-year entitlement.

Where the election actually bites

The two regimes are not interchangeable. The previous Aatmanirbhar schemes ran on the taluka classification of November 2020, which used three categories across 251 talukas; this policy uses two categories across 268. A site that sat in a favourable category under the old classification may sit in Category B under the new one, and the reverse is equally possible.

For any client with a project commissioning between now and late 2027, the election is therefore a modelling exercise rather than a formality, and it has to be done before March 2027 because it cannot be revisited. interpretation

02

Who qualifies

Eligibility runs through five tests in sequence. Each one narrows the entitlement, and two of them account for most of the variation in outcome: what the unit makes, and where it stands. The Resolution is also unusually explicit about what happens when a large project misses the employment test, and that provision decides the result for a good deal of Gujarat's capital-intensive base.

1 · Will commercial production begin between 1 June 2026 and 31 May 2031?
A unit already producing before 1 June 2026 is governed only by the previous scheme and cannot opt in. A unit not yet producing may choose between the two, within six months of the Resolution, and that choice is final.
2 · What is the project character?
A new unit, an expansion or a diversification qualifies. Renovation, modernisation, rehabilitation and rationalisation by an existing undertaking do not qualify at all. This is narrower than most state policies and is easy to assume otherwise.
3 · Which sector tier does the product fall in?
Sports goods, toys, footwear, robots or drones are selected thrust 50% / 45% at any size, with no employment test. One of the 16 listed thrust sectors is thrust. Anything else is general 20% / 15%, and cannot reach mega status at any investment level.
4 · What is the investment, and are the jobs there?
₹125 crore or more in plant and machinery is large. ₹1,000 crore with 250 direct jobs, in a thrust sector, is mega. ₹10,000 crore with 3,000 direct jobs, in a thrust sector, is ultra mega. Both job thresholds escalate with investment.
5 · Which taluka category is the site in?
Category A carries the higher band and Category B the lower. The difference is 5 to 10 percentage points of eligible investment, and the power tariff rate doubles on the same split. Section 4 sets out where the industrial map falls.

What happens when a large project misses the jobs test

The Resolution deals with this expressly, and the treatment is a fall-back rather than a qualification. For a capital-intensive plant with low headcount, which describes much of Gujarat's chemical and metals base, this is the provision that decides the outcome. Two restrictions that shape structuring

The two-availment limit

An undertaking may avail incentives a maximum of two times during the operative period of the policy, whether the projects are new, expansion or diversification. Separately, an undertaking that has already availed any incentive for the same gross fixed capital investment under any state scheme or state agency is not eligible here.

The industrial complex provision

Where a unit sets up multiple projects at a single location and maintains separate books of account with separately identifiable fixed capital investment, those projects may apply separately and are treated as individual units for calculating the incentive. Existing common utilities are not counted.

Where these two provisions meet

The two-availment limit attaches to the undertaking. The industrial complex provision allows genuinely separate projects at one location to be treated as separate units. Whether a complex of four projects draws four entitlements or two is not resolved on the face of the Resolution. This is worth a clarification from the department before it is worth designing around, and commercial substance remains the guardrail in either case. interpretation

03

The thrust sectors

Eligibility lists in state policies are usually closed annexures, and most disputes turn on whether a product appears on them. This one is drafted differently in three respects, and together they make a marginal product a case to be argued rather than a door that is shut.

Why the list is open

  • The sub-sectors under each thrust sector are expressly indicative, not exhaustive
  • Entry 17 is a residual clause: any other sector as decided from time to time by the committee chaired by the Industries Commissioner
  • Where a sub-sector is disputed, that committee decides by considering the HSN code and the opinion of sector experts

What follows in practice

There is a named forum and a named test. The HSN code is the harmonised tariff classification already used for customs and GST, so the argument runs on ground that is familiar and documented. The difference between general and thrust is 20% against 35% of eligible investment, which is usually worth making the case for.

Condition precedent: chemical and pharmaceutical units must have a captive effluent treatment plant, or access to a common one.

The five selected thrust sectors

Sports goods and equipment. Toys. Footwear. Robots. Drones. These carry the highest ceiling in the policy at every size class, including the smallest, with no employment test at all. Section 5 sets out the additional support package that goes with them.

04

Where you can build

The Resolution of 8 September 2026 classifies all 268 talukas across 34 districts into Category A, which carries the higher band, and Category B, which carries the lower. It replaces the three-category classification of 251 talukas made in November 2020. Category A is the less industrially developed set, and the consequence is counter-intuitive: Gujarat's best known industrial addresses are almost all on the lower band.

The two destinations that matter

Dholera is the only major serviced industrial destination on the higher band. Becharaji, which anchors the Mandal-Becharaji investment region and the automotive cluster around it, is the only other Category A taluka with an established industrial base.

The state has deliberately priced its established corridor below its undeveloped talukas, because the corridor does not need a subsidy to attract investment. The differential is the instrument being used to populate Dholera and the tribal belt. interpretation

One point to watch within a single region. Mandal taluka sits in Ahmedabad district and is Category B. Becharaji taluka sits in Mehsana district and is Category A. The Mandal-Becharaji investment region straddles both. Plot selection inside one region moves the band.

The differential is worth 5 to 10 percentage points of eligible fixed capital investment with no change to the project. On ₹5,000 crore of eligible investment that is ₹250 crore to ₹500 crore. The power tariff rate also doubles from ₹1 to ₹2 per unit on the same split, so for an electricity-intensive project the two effects compound.

What you get

The distinctive feature of this policy is that the unit assembles its own package. Rather than receiving a fixed combination, it chooses any one or any combination of capital subsidy, interest subsidy and power tariff support, subject to a sub-limit on each component and an overall ceiling on the total. The components sit inside the ceiling and do not add to it.

06

What counts as eligible investment

This definition decides the real value of the entitlement far more than the headline percentage does. Two concepts run through the scheme and conflating them is the common error. Gross fixed capital investment decides eligibility, size class and the length of the investment period. Eligible fixed capital investment is the base the subsidy is calculated on, and it is smaller.

How the final number is fixed. Final eligibility is set at the lower of the gross fixed capital investment actually made, the cost certified in a project completion certificate from the bank or financial institution, and the asset verification report of the Industries Commissionerate. For a self-financed project, the lower of a certificate from the statutory auditor, chartered accountant, chartered engineer or company secretary, and the asset verification report. fact

Counted in

  • New buildings, at the lower of actual cost or the Roads and Buildings Schedule of Rates, which is the state's standard construction rate card and generally sits below real cost
  • Plant and machinery, including captive power generation within the premises, vehicles and material handling equipment used only within the premises, plant for desalination of sea water or purification of water, and pollution control plant including effluent and hazardous waste facilities
  • Technology, design, drawings and patents, limited to 10% of the eligible investment in plant and machinery
  • Diesel generating sets, but only up to 50% of connected electric load or 25 MW, whichever is less
  • Project infrastructure, in full inside the premises and at 20% outside it

Excluded

  • Land and land development cost, entirely
  • Working capital, goodwill, royalty
  • Preliminary and pre-operative expenses
  • Second-hand, leased or rented plant and machinery, whether Indian or imported
  • Capitalised interest
  • Power generation plant other than captive within the premises
  • Rented or leased building, except GIDC premises
  • Design, drawing, consultancy, supervision and third-party inspection fees without technology acquisition
  • Independent power plant, including renewable energy

What counts as project infrastructure

This head is broader than it first appears and is worth reading carefully on any greenfield project. It covers residential colony, dormitory housing, hospital, school and sports facilities for workers and staff, rest rooms, day care and creche. It covers the feeder road from the nearest approach road, dedicated pipelines carrying water, gas or raw material, and the non-refundable deposit paid to the electricity company for transmission from the nearest sub-station. It also covers digitisation of the communication network and telecommunication cabling, a bank or post office building provided free of charge, a training and skill development centre, and transport facilities such as buses carrying workers from nearby villages and towns.

Inside the project premises the full cost counts. Outside it, only 20% counts. Social infrastructure around a plant is therefore being actively subsidised, which is unusual and is worth building into the project design rather than treating as welfare expenditure outside the model.

Term loans

Loans from banks and financial institutions recognised by the Reserve Bank of India qualify, and so do external commercial borrowings sanctioned by overseas institutions. Loans from government financial institutions such as NDDB, GSFS, HUDCO, REC and PFC qualify, and the sanctioning committee may admit others. Only the amount actually disbursed within the eligible investment period counts. Loans from non-banking financial companies do not qualify at all.

The annual ceiling

Each tier carries two annual caps running in parallel. One is a percentage of eligible investment, the other an absolute figure in rupees. The Resolution states in every tier that carry forward of incentive lost to the annual ceiling is not allowed, so entitlement above what can be drawn in a year is lost rather than deferred. On a very large project this is the single largest reduction in the policy, and it does not appear in any headline number.

Taking an ultra mega thrust-sector project in a Category A taluka. The ceiling is 40% of eligible investment over twelve years. The annual cap is 3.5% of eligible investment. The absolute cap is ₹1,250 crore a year.

08

The power decision

Four provisions in the Resolution interact, and read together they create a genuine commercial choice on any electricity-intensive project. The choice is between a one-off capital allowance and a recurring consumption subsidy, and for a high-consumption plant the second can be worth considerably more.

ProvisionEffect
Capital cost of a captive power plant within the premisesCounts as eligible investment
Independent power plant, including renewableExcluded from eligible investment
Power drawn from a distribution company, or renewable power through open accessEarns the power tariff subsidy
Power consumed from the unit's own captive plantEarns no power tariff subsidy
 For an expansion or diversification the power tariff subsidy applies only to additional consumption, and a sub-meter must be installed to claim it. The subsidy is paid against electricity bills or invoices in the name of the industrial unit. fact

Build captive

The plant cost enters the subsidy base, so it earns at the capital subsidy rate once. Every unit of electricity it then generates earns nothing. Suits a project where reliability or isolation from the grid is the driver, or where consumption is modest relative to the capital cost of the plant.

Draw from grid or renewable open access

The generating asset earns nothing, but every unit consumed earns ₹2 in Category A or ₹1 in Category B, for eight to twelve years, up to 20% or 25% of eligible investment depending on tier. The renewable open access limb means this need not mean buying expensive grid power.

The comparison has to be run per project, on a present value basis, because the answer turns on annual consumption, the tier's power sub-cap, the taluka category and the tenure. It is worth building that comparison once as a reusable model, since it will recur on every metals, chemicals, cement and large-load mandate. interpretation

One further point. Because the power tariff rate doubles between Category B and Category A, the siting decision and the power decision are linked. A power-intensive project in a Category A taluka drawing on renewable open access is taking the two most favourable positions in the policy at once.

How the money reaches you

The process runs in four stages, each with its own deadline, and the deadlines are strict. The sanctioning authority is tiered by investment size, and above ₹10,000 crore the decision is taken by a committee chaired by the Industries Minister. Above that sits a High Power Committee chaired by the Chief Minister.

On state goods and services tax

There is no state goods and services tax reimbursement instrument in this policy. Gujarat moved from net value added tax, and then from net state tax reimbursement, to capital subsidy under the 2020 policy, and this scheme continues that approach. Units holding entitlements under the 2015 policy still receive state tax linked assistance, which is why state announcements in 2026 still refer to it, but it is not available to a new unit. The consequence is a real point of difference against states that still run reimbursement models, and it is dealt with in section 13. fact

The High Power Committee

Chaired by the Chief Minister, with the Deputy Chief Minister or Minister (Industries), the Minister (Finance), the Chief Secretary, and the Additional Chief Secretary or Principal Secretary of Industries and Mines, Finance, Revenue, Environment and Forest, Energy and Petrochemicals, and Labour and Employment. The Industries Commissioner is member secretary. It may invite other officers or experts.

Its stated role is to sanction customised packages for mega and ultra mega industries in thrust and selected thrust sectors, to resolve interdepartmental issues, to facilitate relocation of industries from other states, to monitor projects, and to remove difficulties in implementation.

The residual clause

The Resolution provides that any matter not expressly provided for, or any matter pertaining to the overall industrial development and strengthening of the state's industrial ecosystem, shall be referred to the High Power Committee, whose decision shall be final and binding. A body chaired by the Chief Minister with express authority over anything the Resolution does not cover is the strongest single signal in the document. For a project large enough to reach that room, the published slabs are a floor. fact

Conditions and clawback

The conditions attaching to this entitlement are continuing rather than one-off, and the recovery provision is severe. They warrant active monitoring through the life of the incentive rather than an annual review.

ConditionWhat it requires, and what follows
Local employmentAt least 85% of the total number of employees in all categories must be domiciled in Gujarat, and at least 60% of those employed in managerial and supervisory capacity. This runs throughout the incentive period. For a project bringing in specialist or expatriate technical staff it is the 85% overall test that binds, not the 60% managerial one. It is worth modelling against the hiring plan before commissioning.
Pollution controlThe applicable certificate from the Gujarat Pollution Control Board must be submitted to the Industries Commissioner and kept current.
Continuous productionRequired until the eligible incentive period expires. Where production is discontinued for reasons beyond the control of management, the sanctioning authority may condone the period after verifying the details and satisfying itself as to the reasons.
Recovery on breachOn breach of any one or more conditions, the incentives disbursed are recoverable as an arrear of land revenue, with interest at 18% a year running from the date of first availment, and the undisbursed balance is forfeited. Recovery as a land revenue arrear is a powerful mechanism, and because the interest runs from first availment rather than from the breach, a breach found late is considerably more expensive than the entitlement was worth.
Project characterInvestment by an existing undertaking for renovation, modernisation, rehabilitation or rationalisation is not eligible.
Change of productThe undertaking may add new products or change its products using the existing plant and machinery, with or without modification, during the period of availment, subject to approval of the sanctioning authority.
Provident fundReimbursement runs only for a new employee, defined as a person who did not hold a Universal Account Number before joining and who joins during the incentive period. On an expansion, only the incremental count beyond the pre-existing one. Not available for any period in which the same reimbursement is drawn under a central or state scheme.
ReviewThe Government may conduct a periodic review of the scheme to evaluate implementation and effectiveness, and take appropriate decisions. No stability clause appears in the Resolution.
 fact A dedicated Project Implementation Unit is to be established for coordination, facilitation and monitoring, funded from the same budget head.

Two points worth isolating

The provident fund test is restrictive. Requiring that an employee held no Universal Account Number before joining excludes anyone previously in formal provident-fund-covered employment. On a plant staffed largely by experienced hires the reimbursement is worth materially less than the headline 100% suggests, and the value should be modelled off the expected share of genuinely first-time formal employees rather than off headcount.

Change of ownership is not addressed. The Resolution says nothing about merger, demerger, slump sale, or whether the entitlement transfers with the business. This is the largest open question in the policy, because it determines whether an entitlement is an asset capable of being valued in a transaction. It would fall to the Minister-chaired interpretation committee, or to the High Power Committee under its residual clause. interpretation

Stacking with other schemes

This is the structural point that governs how the whole Gujarat incentive position should be approached. Central schemes stack. Gujarat's own sectoral schemes do not.

CombinationPosition
This scheme plus a central government schemePermitted. Capped so that total incentives received from state and central schemes do not exceed eligible fixed capital investment
This scheme plus another Gujarat sector-specific schemeChoose one. An enterprise eligible under this scheme or any other sector-specific state scheme may opt for either. If it is availing incentives for the same component under another state scheme it is not eligible here, unless specifically provided

 fact

The cap against central schemes is generous as these things go, and leaves real headroom above a 35% state entitlement. It is the choose-one rule against Gujarat's own sectoral policies that constrains. For a project that could sit under the state's electric vehicle, electronics, semiconductor, data centre or renewable energy policies, the general scheme and the sectoral scheme have to be valued against each other and one of them given up.

That makes the state sectoral layer a genuine decision rather than an addition, and it means a sectoral policy should be read for its whole package, not only for whichever instrument looks most generous in isolation.

Customs, foreign trade policy, special economic zone status and bonded manufacturing would change the answer for an export-oriented project. They sit outside the scope of this reference and are flagged for separate consideration.

The payment record

In October 2026 the state approved final eligibility certificates for 25 industrial units covering about ₹3,903 crore of investment and roughly 8,178 expected jobs, at a committee chaired by the Deputy Chief Minister. Of the 25, 22 were still under the Industrial Policy 2015. market view

Three things follow. The machinery runs: certificates are being issued and committees are meeting. The tail is long: a policy from 2015 was still producing final eligibility certificates in late 2026, so an entitlement in Gujarat is a ten-year relationship and the claim function should be staffed for that. And legacy state tax linked incentives are still being paid to units under the earlier policies, on instruments no longer available to a new unit.

What is not yet known

Gujarat's claim-to-cash time and its arrears have not been quantified. A revenue-surplus state with a top-ten departmental allocation is a reasonable starting assumption, and the October 2026 approvals show the machinery working, but neither is evidence of how long a claim takes to become cash. Until that is measured from the Demands for Grants, assembly answers and audit reports, Gujarat is carried at medium on certainty of payment in any state comparison, not high. interpretation

Land and power

Land

  • Land cost is excluded from eligible investment, so land earns no subsidy
  • GIDC allotment in notified estates is the main route
  • Stamp duty and registration reimbursed in full, but only for ultra mega units and selected thrust sectors, and only on land acquired during the investment period
  • Rented buildings are excluded from eligible investment except GIDC premises, which is a deliberate nudge toward GIDC estates

Power

  • ₹2 per unit in Category A and ₹1 per unit in Category B, subject to the component cap
  • Available on supply from a distribution company and on renewable power through open access
  • Not available on consumption from the unit's own captive plant
  • Full exemption from electricity duty under the Gujarat Electricity Duty Act 1958
  • Captive plant capital cost is eligible investment; independent power plant, including renewable, is not

Where the value sits

Five levers, in the order we would test them on a live project.

#LeverWhat it turns on
1Selected thrust at any scaleSports goods, toys, footwear, robots and drones carry the top ceiling at every size class with no employment gate, plus a support package worth up to ₹50 crore on a design studio alone. The most generous position in the policy and the easiest to reach.
2Grid or renewable open access against captiveCompare a one-off capital allowance on a captive plant against a recurring ₹2 or ₹1 per unit on every unit consumed for eight to twelve years. The renewable open access limb means this is not a choice between subsidy and cheap power.
3Treat siting as an incentive decisionWhere a project has genuine locational freedom, Dholera and Becharaji are the only established destinations on the higher band. Where it does not, Category B is the price of being in the corridor and the argument moves to the customised package. Within Mandal-Becharaji, check the plot.
4Argue the product into a thrust sectorIndicative sub-sectors, a residual clause and determination on the HSN code by a named committee mean a marginal product is a case to be made. The difference between general and thrust is 20% against 35%.
5Take anything above ₹1,000 crore to the High Power CommitteeThe Chief Minister's committee can sanction customised packages for mega and ultra mega projects in thrust and selected thrust sectors, and has express authority to decide any matter the Resolution does not cover. It is also mandated to facilitate relocation from other states.
