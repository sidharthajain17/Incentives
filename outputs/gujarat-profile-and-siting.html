Gujarat — state profile and the siting question

The certainty half of the state decision · Companion to the VGIP 2026 decode · Prepared 13 November 2026

The takeaway

The siting finding changes the advice. All 268 talukas are split into Category A (130, higher slab) and Category B (138, lower slab), and Category A is the less developed set. The consequence is counter-intuitive: almost every established industrial destination in Gujarat sits in Category B, on the lower slab — Dahej, Ankleshwar, Jhagadia, Sanand, Mundra, Anjar, Gandhidham, Morbi. Dholera is the one major destination on the top band.

Why the state did this. Gujarat has priced its established corridor below its undeveloped talukas, because the corridor does not need a subsidy to attract investment. The differential is the instrument being used to populate Dholera and the tribal belt. interpretation

On certainty of payment. The machinery demonstrably runs, and the state is in revenue surplus with industries ranked ninth of all departments by allocation. But the payment record has not been quantified, so Gujarat is carried at medium, inferred on certainty in any comparison matrix — not high.

What this is worth, and the trap

The A/B gap is 5 to 10 percentage points of eligible fixed capital investment with no change to the project. On ₹5,000 crore of eligible investment that is ₹250 crore to ₹500 crore. The ₹2 versus ₹1 power tariff differential rides on the same split, which doubles that instrument's value in Category A and compounds the Dholera advantage for an electricity-intensive project.

The trap: a client who assumes Dahej or Sanand carries the top band because those are Gujarat's flagship industrial addresses will plan on the wrong number. interpretation

The practical reading. Gujarat is not bidding from weakness and does not need to overpay. That shows in the policy — its ceilings sit below what some competing states offer on headline percentage. Gujarat sells certainty, infrastructure and speed rather than the biggest number. Any pitch that argues Gujarat on headline rate alone is arguing on the state's weakest ground. interpretation

Certainty of payment

The most informative single data point

October 2026. Final eligibility certificates approved for 25 industrial units covering about ₹3,903 crore of investment and roughly 8,178 expected jobs, at a committee chaired by the Deputy Chief Minister. Of the 25, 22 were under the Industrial Policy 2015. market view

Three things follow:

  1. The machinery runs. Certificates are being issued and committees are meeting.
  2. The tail is long. A 2015 policy was still producing final eligibility certificates in late 2026. An incentive entitlement in Gujarat is a ten-year relationship, not a three-year one — staff the claim function for that.
  3. Legacy state GST-linked incentives are still being paid to units under the earlier policies, on instruments that no longer exist for new units.

Policy continuity. Successive industrial policies in 2009, 2015, 2020 and 2026 with no visible break in entitlements across transitions or across changes of Chief Minister. market view

One structural change is still misunderstood in the market: the 2020 policy delinked incentives from state GST and moved to capital subsidy, and the 2026 policy continues that. Gujarat has been out of the state GST reimbursement model for six years. interpretation

What is missing, stated plainly

Gujarat's payment record has not been quantified. A revenue-surplus state with a top-ten departmental allocation is a reasonable prior, and the October 2026 approvals show the machinery working — but neither is evidence of how long claims take to turn into cash, or what is in arrears.

Until that is in, Gujarat is carried at medium, inferred on certainty of payment in any state comparison matrix. Not high.

Land and power

Land

  • Land cost is excluded from eligible investment, so land earns no subsidy
  • GIDC allotment in notified estates is the main route
  • Government land on lease at 6% of market rate for up to 50 years — under the 2020 policy; whether it carries into 2026 is to verify
  • Stamp duty and registration reimbursed 100%, but only for Ultra Mega and selected thrust units, claimable within three months of production start
  • Rented buildings excluded from eligible investment except GIDC premises — a deliberate nudge toward GIDC estates

Power

  • Power tariff support of ₹2 per unit in Category A, ₹1 in Category B, capped as a percentage of eligible investment
  • 100% electricity duty exemption under the Gujarat Electricity Duty Act 1958
  • Captive power plant cost is eligible investment
  • Independent power plants are excluded
  • Connected load thresholds, open access and renewable banking rules not yet gathered

For power-heavy sectors — metals, chemicals, data-centre-adjacent loads — the power tariff component is likely the highest present-value instrument in the policy, because it arrives through operating life rather than a ten-year claim cycle.
