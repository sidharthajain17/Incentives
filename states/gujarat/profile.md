# Gujarat: state profile

Institutions, people, track record, land and power. The half of the state-selection decision that the policy document does not answer.

Rulebook test for state selection: **effective yield × certainty of payment**. The decode in `industrial-policy-2026.md` carries the yield. This file carries the certainty.

## 2. Institutions

| Body | Role | What it decides for an investor |
|---|---|---|
| **Industries and Mines Department** | Policy owner | Issues the policy and every Government Resolution under it |
| **Industries Commissionerate**, Gandhinagar | Executive arm | Registration, provisional and final eligibility certificates, quarterly claims for large units |
| **District Industries Centres** | District arm | The MSME route — registration and claims for micro, small and medium units |

| **GIFT City** | Financial services zone | Separate regime; outside the industrial policy |

### Sanctioning structure

This is tiered, and the tier decides the speed and the room for negotiation.

| Investment | Authority | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Up to INR 1,000 Cr | Committee, composition to be confirmed | Routine |
| INR 1,000 Cr to INR 10,000 Cr | Committee, composition to be confirmed | Routine but slower |
| Above INR 10,000 Cr | Committee chaired by the Industries Minister | Political attention |
| Customised packages — Mega and Ultra Mega in thrust and selected thrust sectors | **High Power Committee chaired by the Chief Minister** | The real room. Empowered to decide matters the Government Resolution does not cover |
| **SCALE Committee** | Constituted under the 2026 policy; mandate and composition not identified | *(to verify)* |

Two points worth carrying into a meeting:

**Industries sits with the Deputy Chief Minister.** That is a higher political placement than in most states, and the same person chairs the incentive approvals committee. Decisions on incentives are being taken at deputy-CM level rather than departmental level. *(interpretation)*

**The Industries Commissioner is the operative officer** for a large unit. Registration, both eligibility certificates and the quarterly claim all run through that office, not through the district.

## 4. Track record and certainty of payment

### What is actually happening

**October 2026.** Final eligibility certificates approved for 25 industrial units covering about INR 3,903 Cr of investment and roughly 8,178 expected jobs, at a committee chaired by the Deputy Chief Minister. **Of the 25, 22 were under the Industrial Policy 2015.** *(market view — Industries and Mines Department statement, reported October 2026)*

That single fact carries more information than any policy clause:

1. **The machinery runs.** Certificates are being issued and committees are meeting.
2. **The tail is long.** A policy from 2015 was still producing final eligibility certificates in late 2026. An incentive entitlement in Gujarat is a ten-year relationship, not a three-year one. Plan the claim function accordingly.
3. **Legacy state GST-linked incentives are still being paid.** The same statement refers to assistance linked to net state GST, interest subsidy and EPF reimbursement under the two earlier policies — instruments that no longer exist for new units. *(interpretation)*

### Budget capacity

| Measure | FY 2026-27 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total state budget | INR 4,08,053 Cr, up 10.2% | Budget presented 18 Feb 2026 |
| Budget position | Surplus, about INR 974 Cr | Budget speech |
| Capital expenditure | Up 17.5%, about INR 23,469 Cr | Budget speech |
| **Industries and Mines Department allocation** | **INR 13,941.74 Cr — 3.42% of the budget, ranked 9th of all departments** | Department-wise allocation table |

*(market view — reported from the budget documents; the incentive line item within the department allocation has not yet been isolated and should be pulled from the Demands for Grants.)*

A revenue-surplus state running a top-ten departmental allocation for industries is a reasonable position from which to expect payment. That is a prior, not evidence.

### Policy continuity

Successive industrial policies in 2009, 2015, 2020 and 2026, without a visible break in entitlements across transitions, and across changes in Chief Minister. *(market view)*

One structural change is worth recording because it is still misunderstood in the market: **the 2020 policy delinked incentives from state GST and moved to capital subsidy.** The 2026 policy continues that. Gujarat has been out of the state GST reimbursement model for six years. *(interpretation)*

### What is still missing

The honest position: **Gujarat's payment record has not been quantified.** What is needed, and from where:

| Gap | Source to use |
|---|---|
| Incentive line item and actual spend against it | Demands for Grants and Annual Financial Statement, Finance Department |
| Pending and arrear incentive claims | State assembly questions and answers |
| Audit findings on incentive administration | CAG reports on Gujarat, Revenue and Civil |
| Claim-to-cash elapsed time | Credit rating rationales and annual reports of units holding Gujarat entitlements |
| Customised packages granted and their terms | Vibrant Gujarat MoU records, company disclosures — feeds `deals/` |

Until these are in, the certainty-of-payment column in any state comparison matrix is marked **medium, inferred** for Gujarat, not high.

## 5. Land, and the taluka point that changes siting

### The finding

The taluka classification Government Resolution of 8 September 2026 splits all 268 talukas across 34 districts into **Category A (130 talukas, higher slab)** and **Category B (138 talukas, lower slab)**. Category A is the less industrially developed set.

**The consequence is counter-intuitive and commercially large: almost every established industrial destination in Gujarat sits in Category B, on the lower slab.**

| Destination | Taluka | Category | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dholera SIR** | Dholera, Ahmedabad | **A — higher slab** | The one major destination on the top band |
| Dahej / PCPIR | Vagra, Bharuch | B | Lower slab |
| Ankleshwar | Ankleshwar, Bharuch | B | Lower slab |
| Jhagadia | Jhagadia, Bharuch | B | Lower slab |
| Sanand | Sanand, Ahmedabad | B | Lower slab |
| Mundra | Mundra, Kachchh | B | Lower slab |
| Anjar / Gandhidham | Anjar, Gandhidham, Kachchh | B | Lower slab |
| Morbi ceramic cluster | Morbi, Wankaner, Tankara, Halvad | B | Lower slab |
| Ahmedabad, Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot, Bhuj | City talukas | B | Lower slab |
| Tribal belt — Netrang, Dediapada, Sagbara, Garudeshwar, Tilakwada | Bharuch, Narmada | **A** | Higher slab |
| Rapar, Nakhatrana, Lakhpat, Mandvi | Kachchh | **A** | Higher slab |
| Dhandhuka, Detroj-Rampura | Ahmedabad | **A** | Higher slab |
| Maliya | Morbi | **A** | Higher slab |

*(interpretation — compiled from three independent reproductions of Annexure-A to the Government Resolution, which agree with each other and with the text visible in the government-hosted copy. One further source disagreed, placing Vagra, Morbi, Mundra and Anjar in Category A; that source conflicts with the government text and is not followed. **Confirm against the full Annexure before relying on it for a specific site.**)*

### Why this matters more than the headline rate

The gap between Category A and Category B is **5 to 10 percentage points of eligible fixed capital investment**, with no change to the project. On a INR 5,000 Cr eligible investment, that is INR 250 Cr to INR 500 Cr.

A client who assumes Dahej or Sanand carries the top band because those are Gujarat's flagship industrial addresses will plan on the wrong number. The state has deliberately priced the established corridor *below* the undeveloped talukas, because the corridor does not need the subsidy to attract investment. *(interpretation)*

**The strategic read:** Gujarat is using the incentive differential as the instrument to populate **Dholera** and the **tribal belt**. For a greenfield project with real locational freedom, Dholera is the only destination that combines the top incentive band with serviced industrial infrastructure. For a project tied to the chemical corridor or the Kutch ports, Category B is simply the price of being there, and the argument moves to the customised package.

### GIDC and land cost

Land cost is **excluded** from eligible fixed capital investment under the 2026 scheme, so land does not earn subsidy. It is dealt with separately:

- **GIDC allotment** in notified estates
- **Government land on lease** — under the 2020 policy, at 6% of market rate for up to 50 years. Whether this carries into the 2026 policy is *to verify*
- **Stamp duty and registration reimbursement** — 100%, but only for Ultra Mega units and selected thrust sectors, and claimable within three months of commencement of commercial production
- Rented buildings are excluded from eligible investment **except GIDC premises**, which is a deliberate nudge toward GIDC estates *(interpretation)*

## 6. Power

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Distribution | GUVNL and its distribution companies |
| Power tariff support under VGIP 2026 | INR 2 per unit in Category A talukas, INR 1 per unit in Category B, capped as a percentage of eligible investment |
| Electricity duty | 100% exemption under the Gujarat Electricity Duty Act 1958 |
| Captive power | Capital cost is **eligible** investment |
| Independent power plants | **Excluded** from eligible investment |
| Renewable capacity | Large installed base; renewable capacity addition featured in the FY27 budget *(market view)* |
| Connected load thresholds, open access, renewable banking | **Not yet gathered — to do** |

For Sid's power-heavy sectors — metals, chemicals, data-centre-adjacent loads — the power tariff component is likely the highest present-value instrument in the policy, because it arrives through operating life rather than through a ten-year claim cycle. The INR 2 versus INR 1 differential doubles that instrument's value in Category A, which compounds the Dholera point above.
